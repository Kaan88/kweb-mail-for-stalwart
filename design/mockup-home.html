Home and attention: the final design

One page, replacing the three rounds of design/mockup-bento.html. Home is variant A (the owner's pick) without assistant tiles; the assistant comes in through one small sparkle button that a local, non-AI attention layer switches on when something in the cache is worth a look. Crimson Ward palette, light and dark, 360 x 780 screens. Nothing here is built.

1. The phone watches.After each sync a few plain rules in the core read the cache (agenda, tasks, Inbox headers and previews). No model, no network, nothing leaves the phone.
2. The sparkle asks.When a rule fires, a small sparkle with a count appears inside the tile, top bar or header it concerns. No pop-up, no notification, nothing runs.
3. The service answers on a tap.The sparkle shows what was found. Plain buttons act on the phone; a button with a sparkle in front of its verb sends exactly the items it names to the chosen service. A change is made only by Add on the card that shows it.

The rules of the attention layer

Every rule reads only the account's own cache: never a shared account, never Spam or Trash, never encrypted mail (its text is not in the cache). A finding has a stable key; swiped away, it stays away (like a swiped notification); when the data changes so the rule no longer holds (the event moved, the task was ticked, the mail was answered), it disappears by itself. Phone needs no service; Service needs the assistant; Both has a phone action and an assistant action. Without an assistant set up, a finding shows its phone actions only, and a finding that has none is not shown.

The thresholds (2 h, 5 events, 5 tasks, 2 days, 15 min) are fixed defaults (open question 3). The date and keyword patterns are English first (open question 4). The rules run in milliseconds over SQLite after each sync and at midnight; there is no background run of the assistant at all.

Open questions for the owner

  1. Count or dot? The sparkle carries the number of findings (2, 3). A plain dot is calmer but says less. I drew the count.
  2. "Ask before each run" on by default means two taps per run after the first consent (the button, then Send). Keep it on, or on only for runs that send mail (Add dates, Reply ideas) and off for calendar-only runs?
  3. Thresholds: keep 2 h, 5 events, 5 tasks, 2 days, 15 min fixed, or put them in Settings, Home and attention, under each family?
  4. Languages for the date and keyword patterns: English only at first, or English plus the languages you read mail in (which ones)?
  5. Mail outside Home: with Home off (the default), mail findings show only in the thread. Should the mail list mark those rows with the same small ✦ beside the time (no button, no count)?
