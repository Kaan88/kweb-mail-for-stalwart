kweb mail for Stalwart

An Android client built for Stalwart servers only. Second design pass, made while the reading milestone (M3) was built: eleven screens in the Ward colours, in light and dark, each marked with where it stands. All names and messages are examples.

Reading mail

Milestone M3. These five are what the app does today on the emulator, apart from the buttons marked for later.

Notifications and delivery

Milestone M2, the first priority of the project. Unchanged from the first pass.

Still to come

Sending is next (M4, the 0.1.0 release). Calendar and contacts follow. The overview is a proposal, not a commitment.

What changed since the first pass

  • The message screen became a conversation. The first pass showed one mail; a thread of four had nowhere to go. Messages are now cards that fold, with your own replies in between.
  • Folders, search and the remote-images bar were missing although the captions promised them. They are drawn now, as built.
  • Rows carry a star and a clip, so the Flagged and Files chips have something to point at, and a swipe shows what it will do and offers Undo.
  • Delivery check moved into the folder drawer with its state in one word, instead of a bell icon that looked like a notification setting.
  • The scope table follows the plan again: filters and S/MIME or OpenPGP are planned (phase 4) and the AI assistant is in phase 3; the first pass had them as not planned or did not have them.
  • Every screen says where it stands (built, planned, optional), so the page cannot be read as a promise of what is installed.

Scope against Stalwart 1.1

Every row is something Stalwart 1.1.0 advertises over JMAP. Phase 1 (milestones M0 to M4) is the app you can use daily; later phases add to it without changing its structure. Reading, the second row, is what M3 built.

How it is put together

Rust coreJMAP sync, the local cache, sending, and the rule that decides which notifications should exist. Shared with nothing on screen, tested on the desktop.
Kotlin shellJetpack Compose screens, the connection service, Android notifications and their actions. It asks the core what to show and never talks to the server itself.
The bridgeUniFFI generates the Kotlin bindings from the Rust interface, so the two sides cannot drift apart.
